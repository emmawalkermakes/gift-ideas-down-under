Work Secret Santa is a strange little social puzzle. You might draw your manager, someone from another team you've spoken to twice, or the colleague who sits across from you and only drinks hot water. The gift needs to be friendly but not personal in the wrong way, useful but not boring, and it has to come in under the budget, which in many Australian workplaces is around $20 to $30.

Here's a practical guide to getting it right, with personalised ideas that work for almost anyone and a short list of things to steer clear of.

## The golden rules of work Secret Santa

1. **Stay inside the budget.** Overspending makes other people look stingy, which isn't kind.
2. **Keep it workplace-appropriate.** If you'd be uncomfortable explaining it to HR, don't buy it.
3. **Avoid assumptions.** Not everyone drinks alcohol, eats sugar, celebrates Christmas, or has a "World's Best Dad" mug-shaped hole in their life.
4. **Useful beats funny.** Gag gifts get a laugh for 30 seconds and then go in the bin.
5. **Personalise lightly.** A name or initial is great. Anything about their appearance, age or private life is not.

## Personalised Secret Santa ideas under $30

### A name tumbler or keep cup

An insulated tumbler with someone's name or initials is useful in an office, a workshop, a classroom or a ute. It also solves the eternal "whose cup is this?" problem in the staff kitchen. Look for one with a lid that actually seals and check whether it's dishwasher-safe.

### A personalised desk notebook

A good hardcover notebook with their initials on the cover is practical for almost any role. Dot-grid pages suit people who sketch or plan; lined pages suit people who take meeting notes.

### A monogrammed desk mat or mouse pad

A simple felt or leather-look desk mat with an initial makes a desk feel less generic. Keep the design neutral so it suits any setup.

### A personalised lanyard or ID card holder

In workplaces where people wear passes all day, a nicer holder with their name is a small daily upgrade. Check the clip type matches what's standard in your office.

### Name stickers or labels for their gear

For someone who's always lending out chargers and pens, a set of durable name labels is surprisingly well received. It's a quiet "I've noticed you" kind of gift.

### A custom phone stand

A small timber or acrylic phone stand with a name or initial is handy for video calls and keeps a phone off a cluttered desk.

## Non-personalised ideas that suit almost anyone

- **A desk plant that's hard to kill.** A small snake plant, ZZ plant or pothos copes with air-conditioning and neglect. Add a note on watering.
- **Good coffee or tea from a local roaster**, but only if you know they drink it.
- **A quality pen.** Not a luxury brand, just one that writes smoothly.
- **A small desk fan** for workplaces where the air-con loses the battle every February.
- **A cooling neck wrap or quality sunscreen** for outdoor workers in a Queensland summer.

## What to avoid

- **Alcohol**, unless your workplace has explicitly said it's fine and you know the person drinks.
- **Body products and fragrances.** Allergies and sensitivities are common, and it can come across as a comment.
- **Anything about weight, age or looks.** Even as a joke.
- **Novelty items that end up as landfill.** If you wouldn't keep it, they probably won't either.
- **Very religious or very political items.** Keep it neutral.

## If you barely know the person

It's very common to draw someone you don't know well. A few low-key ways to find out more:

- **Look at their desk.** Plants, photos of a dog, a stack of novels, a footy team mug? That's your starting point.
- **Ask a colleague who works with them** what coffee they drink or what they're into.
- **Check the wish list.** Some workplaces ask everyone to name one or two things they'd like. If there's a list, use it.
- **When in doubt, go consumable or practical.** A notebook with their initials and a nice pen is rarely wrong.

## Timing for personalised gifts

Office Secret Santa swaps usually happen at the end-of-year lunch or the last week before people go on leave, which in many workplaces is mid-December. That's earlier than Christmas Day, so work backwards from *that* date, not the 25th.

Personalised items need making time plus postage. Order by the end of November to be comfortable. If your swap is in the week of 14 December and you're relying on standard Parcel Post, don't leave it until the official Australia Post Christmas cut-offs, because those are aimed at Christmas Day delivery. You can check the [official Australia Post Christmas sending dates](https://auspost.com.au/personal/sending/christmas-sending-dates) for the latest information.

## Wrapping it up

Keep the card friendly and light: "Hope this makes the 3pm slump a bit better" is perfect. Don't sign it if your workplace keeps the giver secret, and make sure the name on the gift tag is the recipient's, not yours.
