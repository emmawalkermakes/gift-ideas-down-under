End-of-year gifts for teachers sit in an awkward spot. You want something that feels personal, you don't want to look like you're trying too hard, and you're usually shopping in the same fortnight as every other family in the class. A budget around $30 keeps things generous without tipping into awkward territory — and for most teachers, the thoughtful bit matters more than the price tag.

This is a practical guide for Australian families, with Queensland Term 4 timing in mind. The same ideas work for childcare educators, coaches and music tutors if you adjust the calendar.

## When to give it (Queensland Term 4)

Queensland state school Term 4 in 2026 runs from Tuesday 6 October to Friday 11 December, according to the [Department of Education term dates](https://education.qld.gov.au/about-us/calendar/term-dates). That last Friday is the usual end of year for most year levels — but it is not a guarantee for every teacher you know.

Year 12 students often finish earlier for Senior Statement processes. Teachers' calendar notes for 2026 list around 20 November for Year 12 final attendance for the Senior Statement. If your child is in Year 12, or you're shopping for a senior-school teacher, **check your school's published last day** rather than assuming 11 December.

A few timing rules that save stress:

- **Give the gift before the teacher's last day**, not on Christmas Day. Most teachers have left the classroom by then, and a gift sitting at home for weeks doesn't land the same way.
- **Aim for the last teaching week**, or the last shared class if your child finishes earlier.
- For **childcare and OSHC**, ask when the educator's last day with your child is — it may not match the school calendar.
- For **coaches and tutors**, the last lesson of the term is usually the right moment. A card on the day of the final session is better than a parcel that arrives in January.

If you're pooling money with other families for a class gift, start the conversation two to three weeks out. Collections that begin on the Monday of the last week tend to leave someone scrambling for change.

## What schools usually prefer (and what to check)

Queensland Department of Education gift procedures treat some gifts differently from others. Cash and Visa/Mastercard-style prepaid cards must be refused. End-of-year appreciation gifts may be accepted under school approval rules — but **those rules are not identical at every school**, and dollar thresholds are not something to quote as if they were universal.

What that means for you as a parent:

1. **Check your school's gift policy** (newsletter, parent handbook, or a quick email to the office) before you spend.
2. Prefer **small, personal appreciation gifts** over anything that looks like a payment or a high-value voucher.
3. When in doubt, a **handwritten note plus a modest consumable** is almost always the safest combination.

Private schools, Catholic schools and early childhood centres set their own policies too. Don't assume the state-school rules apply there — ask.

## Start with the note (seriously)

Before you buy anything, get the child to write or dictate a short note. Teachers remember these long after the tea bags are gone. A few prompts that help kids who freeze at a blank card:

- One thing they learned this year that stuck.
- A moment in class they enjoyed.
- A thank you for something specific (patience with reading, help with maths, making science fun).

Keep it short. A few genuine sentences beat a long paragraph copied from a template. If your child is very young, they can draw a picture and you can add one line underneath in their words.

If you're organising a class gift, still encourage individual notes. A single group card is fine as a backup, but individual messages are what teachers actually keep.

## Ideas that stay under a $30 budget

Think in **categories and methods**, not brand names. Prices move, and a specific product link from November may not exist by December. Shop local where you can, and treat "$30" as a ceiling for the whole gift (including wrapping), not a target you have to hit.

### Consumables they'll actually use

Teachers get a lot of novelty mugs. They get fewer things they can finish and not store forever.

- A small selection of **good tea, coffee or hot chocolate**. Choose based on what you've noticed them drinking, or keep it simple with a popular blend and a short note.
- A bar or two of **decent chocolate**, or a small tin of biscuits, if your school allows food gifts and there are no allergy concerns you're aware of.
- A **hand cream or lip balm** for someone who's always outdoors on duty — Queensland sun and air-conditioning dry people out.

Consumables work well because they don't add to classroom clutter. If you're unsure about allergies or scent sensitivity, skip lotions and stick to sealed tea or coffee.

### A small plant

A hardy indoor plant in a simple pot is a classic for a reason. Choose something forgiving (think hardy foliage rather than a fussy flowering plant), water it lightly before you gift it so it isn't dripping in the car, and include a care note on a luggage tag: light, water, and whether it's okay near air-conditioning.

If the teacher already has a jungle on the windowsill, skip this and go back to consumables. More plants are not always kinder.

### Something useful for the desk — kept small

- A **nice pen** they'll actually write with (not a novelty shape).
- A small **notebook** for planning or to-do lists.
- A **reusable keep-cup** only if you've seen them buy takeaway coffee and you know they don't already have three.

Avoid large decorative items. Classroom storage is limited, and end-of-year packing is already a job.

### The handwritten-plus-small-gift combo

My favourite formula under $30:

1. Child's note (or drawing).
2. One consumable or small plant.
3. Simple wrapping — tissue and ribbon, or a reusable tote they can keep.

It looks considered, stays within budget, and doesn't put the teacher in an awkward position with school policy.

## Pooling for a class gift

Class gifts make sense when twenty families each buying a separate present would overwhelm one person. A few practical tips:

- **One organiser, one message.** Put a note in the class chat with a clear budget per family (for example $5–$10 each) and a deadline.
- **Agree the gift type first.** A shared voucher for a bookshop or a gardening centre, a quality tea hamper, or a contribution toward something the teacher has mentioned wanting for the classroom — but only if school policy allows vouchers of that type. Prepaid Visa/Mastercard-style cards are a problem under the DoE rules mentioned above; a store voucher for a specific retailer is a different category, and you should still confirm with the school.
- **Include an option to give a card only.** Not every family can contribute cash that week, and no one should feel excluded.
- **Present it on a day when the teacher is actually there.** Last-day assemblies can be chaotic; the final shared lesson is often calmer.

## Childcare educators, coaches and tutors

The same principles apply, with a few tweaks:

- **Childcare:** Educators often work with several age groups and may not have a single "last day" that matches school. Ask the centre when gifts are usually given, and whether there is a centre-wide policy.
- **Sports coaches:** A note from the team plus a small shared gift (consumables, or a contribution toward next season's kit if the club allows it) works well. Give it at the last training or presentation evening.
- **Music and tutoring:** The last lesson of the year is the natural moment. A handwritten thank you from the student means more here than almost anything you can buy.

For all of these, keep the gift modest and the note specific. People who work with kids hear a lot of generic praise; a detail about progress or patience stands out.

## What I'd skip

- **Cash or generic prepaid cards** — refuse territory under DoE procedure, and awkward even where policy is looser.
- **Heavily scented candles or diffusers** without knowing the teacher's preferences or classroom rules.
- **Anything that needs assembly, batteries or a manual.** End of term is not the moment.
- **Matching gifts for every specialist teacher** if the budget is tight. Prioritise the people your child saw every day; a sincere note alone is enough for someone they saw once a week.
- **Leaving it until the final morning.** Teachers are packing up rooms, chasing reports and saying goodbye to dozens of families. A gift handed over calmly earlier in the week is easier on everyone.

## A simple plan for the next fortnight

1. Check your school's last day and gift policy.
2. Get the child's note written this weekend while there's still time to think.
3. Decide: individual small gift, or join a class pool.
4. Buy the consumable or plant mid-week, wrap simply, and deliver before the last day.

You don't need a perfect present. You need something kind, within a sensible budget, and timed for when the teacher is still there to receive it. Under $30 is plenty for that — especially when the note is real.
