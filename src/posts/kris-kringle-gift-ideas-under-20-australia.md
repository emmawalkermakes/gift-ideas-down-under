A $20 Kris Kringle limit is the most common cap in Australian families, friendship groups and sports clubs, and it's also the hardest one to shop for. Twenty dollars buys you a box of chocolates that melts in the car, a novelty mug nobody asked for, or a candle that sits in a cupboard until next December.

The trick is to stop trying to buy something *impressive* for $20 and instead buy something *specific*. A small, useful item with a name, an initial or an in-joke on it will nearly always beat a bigger generic gift. This guide is built around that idea, with a few non-personalised fall-backs for when time runs out.

## First, a few ground rules for a $20 budget

- **Spend on the thing, not the packaging.** A $6 gift bag eats almost a third of your budget. Brown paper, kitchen string and a sprig of rosemary or bottlebrush from the garden looks better anyway.
- **Think about heat.** Christmas Day in most of Australia is warm to hot. Chocolate, lip balm, wax candles and anything with soft plastic can warp or melt if it sits in a car or under a tree in a hot lounge room.
- **Personalisation takes time.** Even simple custom items usually need a few days to make before they're posted. If you're ordering online, start in November rather than mid-December.
- **Ask about allergies and dietary needs** before you buy anything edible. It's an easy question to slip into the group chat.

## Personalised Kris Kringle ideas under $20

### 1. A name-printed stubby holder

It's not glamorous, but it's genuinely useful from November to March, and a name means it doesn't get mixed up at the backyard cricket. If your recipient doesn't drink, it works just as well on a can of soft drink or sparkling water. Look for neoprene with a stitched base, which lasts longer than the thin foam versions.

### 2. A personalised bookmark

Bookmarks are flat, light and cheap to post, which makes them ideal if your Kris Kringle is spread across states. Timber, leather and acrylic versions all exist; a first name or a short line from a favourite book is plenty. Pair it with a second-hand paperback by an author they like and you're still under budget.

### 3. A monogrammed tea towel

Tea towels are one of those things everyone uses and nobody buys for themselves. A single initial or a family surname looks more grown-up than a cartoon. Choose a cotton-linen blend; pure polyester "souvenir" tea towels look nice and don't dry anything.

### 4. Custom photo magnets

A set of three or four fridge magnets made from phone photos is a nice one for grandparents or anyone who's hard to buy for. Choose photos that are sharp and well lit, with faces filling a good part of the frame. Tiny faces in a big landscape shot get lost at magnet size.

### 5. An initial keyring

Simple, cheap and a good choice for teenagers who have just got their L or P plates, or someone who's moved into a new place this year. A single letter or a short word is more durable than a long name, which can wear off with keys rubbing against it.

### 6. A personalised luggage tag

If your recipient travels for work or is heading off on a summer holiday, a sturdy luggage tag with their initials is useful and makes their bag easier to spot on the carousel. Avoid putting a full home address on the outside; a name and mobile number is enough.

### 7. A labelled pencil case or desk tidy for a kid

If your family Kris Kringle includes children, something with their name for the new school year is practical. The Queensland school year starts in late January, so it'll get used straight away rather than forgotten.

## Non-personalised ideas that still feel thoughtful

Sometimes there isn't time to order anything custom. These work well under $20 if you add a handwritten note:

- **A nice hot sauce, local honey or a jar of chutney** from a farmers market. Check the use-by date and keep it out of the sun.
- **A good-quality sunscreen stick or SPF lip balm.** Boring? Maybe. Used? Absolutely.
- **Seeds and a small pot.** Basil, cherry tomatoes and chillies all handle Queensland summers well.
- **A paperback from a second-hand bookshop**, ideally one you've read and can recommend in a sentence.
- **A deck of cards or a small travel game** for a family that's heading away over the holidays.

## How to make a cheap gift look considered

The difference between "gift" and "obligation" is usually in the last 10 minutes of effort:

1. **Write one line on the tag.** Not "Merry Christmas from Secret Santa", but a reason: "For the next time you're stuck in traffic on the Gateway."
2. **Wrap it in a way that suits them.** Newspaper comics for a kid, plain kraft paper for someone minimalist.
3. **Bundle small things.** A $9 stubby holder plus a $5 bag of local macadamias feels like a little hamper rather than a single item.

## Timing your order for Christmas

If you're ordering anything personalised online, two clocks are running: the maker's production time and the postage time. Australia Post publishes Christmas cut-off dates every year. For 2026, their published metro Parcel Post dates for sending from Queensland are Monday 21 December for most other states and Tuesday 22 December within Queensland, with earlier dates for WA (Thursday 17 December), Tasmania (Friday 18 December) and the NT (Monday 14 December). Regional addresses need extra time.

Those are the dates *the parcel must be lodged by*, not the date you should order. Add the seller's making time on top, then add a few days of buffer. In practice, ordering personalised Kris Kringle gifts by early December is sensible.

Always check the [official Australia Post Christmas sending dates](https://auspost.com.au/personal/sending/christmas-sending-dates) before relying on them, as they can change.

## Quick checklist

- Confirm the budget (and whether it's "under $20" or "around $20").
- Ask about allergies before buying food.
- Check the spelling of the name, including nicknames and preferred spellings.
- Order personalised items in November or early December.
- Keep anything heat-sensitive out of the car and away from sunny windows.
- Add a one-line handwritten note.
