Every December, a lot of us get the same answer from Dad: "Don't get me anything, I've got everything I need." And every December, we ignore it, buy another novelty barbecue apron and hope for the best.

I've stopped asking the question and started watching instead. A dad who "needs nothing" is almost never short of things; he's short of things he'd buy for himself. That's a different problem, and it has a much better solution than another bottle of something from the airport.

So instead of a list of products, here's a method, followed by ideas sorted by type of dad. Pick the type that sounds most like yours, or a mix.

## The method: look at what he already uses every day

Over a week or two, quietly notice what he reaches for. Then ask yourself one of four questions about it:

1. **Is it worn out?** The wallet with the cracked edge, the thongs held together with hope, the coffee mug with the chip. Replacing something he'd never replace himself is the safest "useful" gift there is.
2. **Is it a cheap version of something he loves?** If he drinks tea or coffee every morning, a really good cup, a better kettle or a grinder is an upgrade he'll use daily.
3. **Could it carry his name or a bit of history?** Personalising doesn't suit everyone, but a dad who keeps his tools or fishing gear labelled will often appreciate a properly marked version.
4. **Is it something he'd enjoy more with company?** Sometimes the gift isn't an object at all.

If none of those apply, move on to the types below.

## The tinkerer (shed, garage, backyard)

This dad probably owns three of every tool and still can't find the one he wants.

- **Storage that fits his actual mess.** Stackable bins, a pegboard kit or a magnetic strip for the wall. Measure a bit of the shed first so it genuinely fits.
- **A tool roll or personalised toolbox tag.** Useful if he carries tools between jobs. Look at what he already takes in the car.
- **A good headlamp or rechargeable work light.** Dads who work on cars, plumbing or anything under a sink tend to use these constantly.
- **A consumables hamper.** Quality sandpaper, a few good drill bits, glues and tapes he'd never think to buy. It's unglamorous, and it's the kind of gift he'll remember each time he needs one.

## The griller (barbecue, outdoor entertaining)

Australian summers mean this dad is on duty most weekends from now until Australia Day.

- **A good set of long tongs and a sturdy spatula.** Sounds boring; he'll use them every time. Skip the novelty ones with bottle openers that never quite work.
- **A meat thermometer.** Practical, and it helps with safe cooking. A simple, reliable one is better than a fancy one.
- **A hot-plate scraper and cleaning set.** He may not buy one, but he'll feel the difference.
- **An engraved or labelled chopping board.** A solid wooden board with a name or short message on it lasts for years and suits the table as well as the barbecue.

## The sporty dad (weekend cricket, golf, running or the surf)

- **Replacements for the worn-out basics.** A new cap, a decent water bottle, a towel for the car. In Queensland, a broad-brimmed hat and sun protection are things most people forget to replace.
- **A personalised kit bag or bottle.** Marking things with his name or club number stops his gear walking off after the weekend game.
- **A ticket or membership.** If he follows a team, a game day out with you is worth more than anything you could wrap.

## The homebody (books, music, TV, coffee)

- **A really good mug or tumbler.** Look at what he drinks from now. If it's a chipped freebie from a conference, you've found your gift. A name or a short in-joke printed on the mug makes it his.
- **A reading light, a comfortable cushion or a throw.** Small comforts he wouldn't buy for himself.
- **A subscription to something he already likes.** A magazine, a streaming service he's mentioned, or an audiobook service. Check what he already has so you don't double up.
- **Photo books.** A printed book of the year's family photos is easy to make, and dads who say they need nothing often keep these on the coffee table for months.

## The dad who loves a trip (caravan, camping, fishing)

- **Camping gear that fixes a small annoyance.** A better torch, a collapsible bucket, a quality pocket knife where it's legal and suitable for his use, a decent chair.
- **A personalised tackle tin or tag.** Fishing and camping gear tends to be shared around, and labelled kit tends to find its way back.
- **A road trip contribution.** If he's planning a drive, a fuel voucher or a night at a caravan park he's been eyeing is a practical present.

## When the answer really is "experience" or "time"

Some dads would honestly prefer an afternoon with you to anything in a box. A few ways to wrap it up so it feels like a present:

- **A date in the diary.** Pick the activity, book it, and put it on a card. A ticket or a booking reference feels like a real gift.
- **A jobs voucher.** "One Saturday of my time to help with whatever you've been putting off." Your dad will take it more seriously than you'd expect.
- **A shared project.** Building a bench, restoring an old tool, planting a veggie patch. You get a result and some time together.

## Things I'd skip

- **Novelty gifts with a joke on them.** They're funny once and then live in a drawer.
- **Anything that requires him to learn a new gadget.** If he needs a manual, he probably won't use it.
- **Anything you can't return if it doesn't fit.** Clothes are risky unless you know the size. Check what he's wearing, or ask someone close to him.
- **Over-personalising.** A name is lovely. A full paragraph of text on a mug is a lot.

## Timing: order early if it's personalised

If you're ordering anything made-to-order or engraved, allow extra time for it to be made and posted. For a plan that works backwards from Australia Post's own dates, see my [Christmas 2026 gift posting timeline](/posts/christmas-gift-posting-timeline-australia-2026/). Anything you can buy in a shop on the day is a safe backup.

## A last tip

If you're really unsure, ask someone who sees him more often: Mum, his mates, his workmate. They'll know whether he's been complaining about the fishing rod, the boots or the broken kettle. And if all else fails, a good gift that he didn't know he wanted is better than the one he asked for. He'll tell you he didn't need it, then use it every day.
