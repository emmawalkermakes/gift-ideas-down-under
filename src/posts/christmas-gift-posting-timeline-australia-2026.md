Every year I tell myself I'll have the interstate parcels sorted by the start of December, and every year at least one of them ends up in a queue at the post office with a week to spare. So this year I've written the plan down, working backwards from the dates that actually matter.

The anchor for everything below is Australia Post's own [Christmas cut-off dates page](https://auspost.com.au/personal/sending/christmas-sending-dates), which I checked on 3 November 2026. Those dates are the *last* day to send for the best chance of arrival before Christmas Day. They're not a guarantee, and Australia Post notes they're a guide that can change, so treat them as the finish line rather than the plan.

## Step one: sort your list by distance, not by person

Most of us write the gift list by person: Mum, my brother, the nephews, my friend in Perth. For posting, re-sort it by destination, because the deadline depends on where the parcel goes.

Make four columns:

1. **Overseas.** These are the earliest deadlines by a long way.
2. **Far interstate.** WA, the NT and Tasmania have earlier cut-offs than the east coast.
3. **Nearby interstate and same state.** The latest cut-offs.
4. **Hand-delivered.** No deadline except your own calendar.

Then, for anything that's personalised, made to order or coming from a small seller, add one more note: how long it takes to *arrive at your place* before you can wrap and re-post it. That lead time is the part people forget.

## Step two: the overseas parcels (late November to mid-December)

International dates depend on the country and the service. Here are the ones most Aussie families seem to need, straight from the Australia Post table:

| Sending to | Economy Air | Standard | Express | Courier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New Zealand | 2 Dec | 9 Dec | 15 Dec | 17 Dec |
| UK | 4 Dec | 11 Dec | 15 Dec | 17 Dec |
| USA | 30 Nov | 7 Dec | 11 Dec | 17 Dec |
| Canada | 1 Dec | 8 Dec | 14 Dec | 17 Dec |
| China | 27 Nov | 4 Dec | 8 Dec | 15 Dec |
| Major European destinations | 4 Dec | 11 Dec | 14 Dec | 15 Dec |

A few things worth knowing from the fine print:

- **International Courier is only available at select outlets and to select destinations**, so don't plan around it without checking.
- **Some countries have services suspended.** The full table on the Australia Post page lists every country; scroll for yours before you buy anything bulky.
- **If you're sending from South Australia, Tasmania or the NT**, Australia Post says to add 2 business days for Express and 5 business days for Standard and Economy, because items travel interstate before leaving the country.
- **Regional and rural senders should allow extra time.** The dates assume metro to metro.

My rule of thumb: if the overseas gift is going economy, I want it wrapped and ready by the third week of November. That leaves room for the inevitable "I forgot to buy the card" detour.

## Step three: far interstate (by mid-December)

Parcel Post cut-offs within Australia are published as a grid of "from" and "to" states. Here's the Queensland row, since this blog is written from Brisbane:

| From Qld (metro) to | Parcel Post cut-off |
|---|---|
| Qld | Tue 22 Dec |
| NSW, ACT, Vic, SA | Mon 21 Dec |
| Tas | Fri 18 Dec |
| WA | Thu 17 Dec |
| NT | Mon 14 Dec |

If you're in another state, find your own row on the [official page](https://auspost.com.au/personal/sending/christmas-sending-dates); the gaps are different. For example, WA to the NT is Monday 14 December, and the NT to NSW, the ACT and Victoria is Wednesday 16 December.

Two practical notes:

- **Over-the-counter Parcel Post and Express Post items must be lodged by 2.30pm** on the cut-off day. Lodging at 4pm on the deadline is effectively lodging a day late.
- **Gold Coast, Geelong and Newcastle follow the metro dates.** Other regional areas need extra time.

## Step four: Express Post as your safety net

Express Post buys you a little more time between metro areas. The cut-off is **Wednesday 23 December 2026** for most of the country, and **Tuesday 22 December** for items going to WA or the NT, or coming from WA. Australia Post also notes that some items, such as dangerous goods or large freight, may not be suitable for air transport, which matters if you're sending something like aerosols, perfume or certain batteries.

I don't plan to use Express. I keep it in my back pocket for the one parcel that inevitably goes wrong.

## Step five: cards and letters

Cards have their own cut-offs: **18 December interstate and 21 December within the same state**, for regular post between metro locations.

At the time of writing, a Christmas card within Australia costs 65c during November and December, provided it goes in a sealed envelope marked "Card Only" and stays within Australia Post's size and weight limits (130 x 240mm, 5mm thick, 250g). A bulky homemade card with a ribbon and buttons glued on can easily blow the thickness limit, so measure it before you buy a sheet of stamps. Cards to anywhere overseas are listed at $3.50 with a "Card Only" envelope, with their own limits.

## Putting it on the calendar

Here's the backwards timeline I'm using for 2026, starting from a Brisbane address. Adjust the dates for your state.

**This week (early November)**
- Re-sort the gift list by destination.
- Order anything personalised, engraved or custom-made that's going overseas. Ask the maker for their current turnaround and whether they need a proof approved first.
- Buy padded mailers and a roll of decent tape before the shops run low.

**Mid-November**
- Order personalised gifts for interstate recipients.
- Confirm addresses. Text people now; it's far less awkward than a returned parcel in January.

**Late November**
- Wrap and post anything going Economy Air overseas (China's Economy Air date is 27 November; the USA's is 30 November).
- Write cards for overseas friends.

**First week of December**
- Post remaining overseas parcels by Standard, aiming well ahead of the 7 to 11 December dates.
- Post anything going to the NT (14 December from Qld) and WA (17 December from Qld).

**Second week of December**
- Post Tasmanian, interstate and same-state parcels. Yes, you technically have until the 21st or 22nd, but post offices get busy in the final week.
- Post interstate cards (18 December).

**Third week of December**
- Same-state cards by 21 December.
- Anything left goes Express, by 22 or 23 December depending on destination.

## Packing so it arrives looking like a gift

A few things I've learned the hard way:

- **Wrap first, then pack.** A wrapped gift in a padded mailer survives better than an unwrapped one in wrapping paper on the outside.
- **Double-check what's inside if it's heat-sensitive.** Parcels can sit in hot vans and depots in an Australian December. Chocolate, candles and some cosmetics don't love that.
- **Put a note with the recipient's name inside the parcel.** If the outer label gets damaged, it gives the parcel a fighting chance.
- **Check the cost before you seal it.** Australia Post lists flat-rate options for parcels up to 5kg within Australia, ranging at the time of writing from $10.20 for an extra small Parcel Post satchel to $32.95 for extra large Express Post. If you use your own box, you're charged on actual or cubic weight, whichever is greater, so a small gift in a huge box costs more than it needs to.
- **Keep the tracking numbers in one note on your phone** with the recipient's name next to each.

## When a gift won't make it

Some years it just doesn't come together. If the date has passed, a few honest options:

- **Send a card that says the gift is coming.** Most people are delighted to have Christmas extend into January.
- **Order directly to their address** from a seller who ships from their state, if the gift is generic enough.
- **Give an experience that doesn't need posting**, like a booking or a subscription, with a printed note in the card.
- **Save it for a "Christmas in July" catch-up.** Not ideal, but it takes the pressure off.

The point of working backwards isn't to make Christmas more stressful. It's to move the stress to early November, when it's cheaper and the post office queue is shorter. If you're still deciding what to send, the [gift finder](/gift-finder/) can help narrow it down by recipient and budget.
