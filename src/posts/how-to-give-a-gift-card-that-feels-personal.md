Gift cards have a reputation as the gift you buy when you've run out of ideas. That's unfair. For a teenager saving for something specific, a friend who's renovating, or a relative interstate whose tastes you don't know well, a gift card can be the most useful thing under the tree.

What makes a gift card feel lazy isn't the card. It's the way it's chosen and handed over. Here's how to get both right, plus a quick check of the rules that protect gift card buyers in Australia.

## Step 1: choose a card for the person, not the shopping centre

The most personal gift cards are specific. Compare:

- "A general shopping centre card" versus "a card for the bookshop she spends hours in".
- "A supermarket card" versus "a card for the hardware store, because he's halfway through the deck".
- "A department store card" versus "a card for the local nursery where they buy every seedling".

A specific shop says "I know what you love". It also tends to be spent, rather than forgotten in a wallet.

**Good options to consider:**

- A local independent business they already visit: a café, bookshop, record store, bike shop, yarn shop or plant nursery.
- A class or experience provider, if you know they've been wanting to try something.
- A small maker whose work they've admired. Many makers sell their own gift vouchers; ask at their market stall or check their website.
- A streaming, music or app store card for someone who mostly lives on their phone, if that's genuinely how they spend their downtime.

**Think twice about:** a card for a shop they'd need to travel a long way to reach, or one that can only be used in-store when they mostly shop online (or the reverse). Check where and how the card can be redeemed before you buy.

## Step 2: check the fine print before you pay

Since 1 November 2019, most gift cards and vouchers sold in Australia must have an expiry period of at least three years, and the expiry date has to be shown on the card. Businesses also can't charge most fees after the card has been sold, such as account-keeping fees. There are some exceptions, including reloadable cards, promotional vouchers and some genuinely discounted cards. The [ACCC's gift card page](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/advertising-and-promotions/gift-cards-and-discount-vouchers) explains the rules and the exemptions.

A few practical checks that the rules don't cover:

- **Purchase fees.** Some prepaid cards, especially multi-store or "use anywhere" cards, charge a fee when you buy them. Check the price at the counter or checkout before you pay.
- **Activation.** Many cards are activated at the register. Keep your receipt until the recipient has used the card, in case it wasn't activated properly.
- **Online versus in-store.** Some cards work only in physical stores, only online, or only at particular locations.
- **Small businesses.** If the business closes or goes into administration, gift cards may not be honoured. For a small local shop that's a reason to encourage the recipient to use it reasonably soon, not a reason to avoid buying.
- **Digital cards.** E-gift cards are handy for last-minute gifts, but double-check the email address you send them to.

## Step 3: add a reason

This is the step that changes how the gift feels. Write a short note on the card or envelope that explains *why this card*:

- "For the next three books on your list. I want to hear about the best one."
- "Towards the new planter box. Happy to come and help put it together."
- "Your morning coffee is on me for a while. Thank you for everything this year."
- "For a pottery class, because you've been talking about it since autumn."

One or two sentences is enough. It turns an amount of money into a plan, and it shows you've listened.

A card handed over in its cardboard sleeve looks like an afterthought. A few low-effort ways to give it some presence:

- **Pair it with a small, related object.** A bookshop card tucked into a bookmark or a secondhand paperback. A café card with a reusable cup. A hardware card tied to a pair of work gloves. A nursery card in a small pot with a seed packet.
- **Hide it inside something.** Folded into a homemade card, inside a box of chocolates, or rolled up in a pair of socks.
- **Wrap it properly.** A small box with tissue paper and ribbon makes the reveal feel like any other gift.
- **For kids and teens, make it a game.** A mini treasure hunt with clues around the house is far more fun than an envelope.

## Step 5: think about the amount (and don't stress it)

There's no rule about how much to put on a gift card. A sensible approach is to load what you would have spent on a physical gift for that person. If you're worried a smaller amount looks stingy, choose a shop where it buys something complete: a coffee card that covers a week of takeaway coffees feels more generous than the same amount at a big department store.

If a group is chipping in, one larger card for something meaningful, such as a course, a piece of equipment or a big-ticket item they're saving for, usually beats several small ones.

## When a gift card is the right choice

Gift cards work especially well for:

- **Teenagers**, who often prefer choosing their own clothes, games or music. Our [teenager gift guide](/posts/christmas-gifts-for-teenagers-australia/) has more on that.
- **People who've just moved**, and need practical things you can't predict.
- **Someone in the middle of a project**, such as a renovation, a garden or a new hobby.
- **Interstate or overseas family**, where posting a parcel is costly or slow. A digital card can arrive on Christmas morning.
- **Anyone who's told you exactly what they're saving for.**

They're a weaker choice when the person is a "sentimental keeper" who treasures objects, or when they'd feel obliged to spend the card at a shop they don't like. In those cases, a small physical gift with a handwritten note will mean more.

## A quick checklist

- [ ] Specific shop or experience they actually use
- [ ] Expiry date and any purchase fee checked
- [ ] Works in the way they shop (online or in-store)
- [ ] Receipt kept until it's been used
- [ ] A one-line note explaining why
- [ ] Wrapped, paired or hidden in something

Done this way, a gift card stops being the "I didn't know what to get you" option, and becomes "I know exactly what you'll do with this".
