Kids' stockings are easy: lollies, a small toy, a book, done. Adult stockings are where good intentions go to die. It's tempting to grab whatever's stacked near the checkout in December, and by February most of it is in a drawer or the bin.

The fix is to treat a stocking as a handful of small, *used-up-or-used-often* things, chosen for one person. This list is grouped by what the item does, so you can mix one or two from each group rather than working through a generic "top 50".

## A simple formula

You don't need a rule, but a loose shape helps. For most adults, a stocking that feels generous has:

1. **Something to eat or drink.** It gets used, and it never clutters a cupboard for long.
2. **Something practical they'll use weekly.** The kind of thing they'd never buy a nice version of for themselves.
3. **Something small for fun.** A game, a puzzle, a silly-but-good item.
4. **One personal touch.** Their name, their hobby, an in-joke.

Four to six items is plenty. A stocking that's half-full of good things beats one stuffed with filler.

## Edible and drinkable

Consumables are the safest stocking fillers there are. A few ideas that travel well and suit an Australian summer:

- **Good coffee or loose-leaf tea** in a small bag or tin, ideally from a local roaster or tea shop.
- **Hot sauce, chilli oil or a spice blend** for the person who adds heat to everything.
- **A jar of local honey.** Honey is one of the few foods that copes happily with a hot Christmas Day.
- **Fancy salt, a dukkah or a pepper grinder refill.**
- **Nuts, dried fruit or a block of good chocolate.** One warning: chocolate in a stocking in a Queensland December needs to live in the fridge until the morning. Wrap it last.
- **Hard lollies or licorice from their childhood.** Nostalgia goes a long way.

## Practical things they'll actually use

The best practical fillers are upgrades of things people use constantly but never think about.

- **A good pair of socks.** Merino for walkers, bright patterns for anyone who likes a talking point. Socks in a stocking is a joke as old as stockings, and people still wear them.
- **Lip balm with SPF and a small sunscreen** for the bag or car. Useful year-round here, not just at the beach.
- **A quality kitchen tool:** a sharp paring knife (for an adult who'll appreciate it), a microplane, a silicone spatula, a jar opener.
- **Reusable produce bags, a collapsible cup or a beeswax wrap.**
- **A phone charging cable or small power bank.** Unglamorous, constantly lost, always appreciated.
- **A notebook and a pen they'll like writing with.**
- **Hand cream** for the gardener or anyone whose hands take a beating.
- **Hair ties, clips or a scrunchie**, if that's their thing.

## Small and fun

This is where a stocking gets its personality. Keep it to one or two items.

- **A deck of cards or a pocket-sized game.** Something that can come out at the Christmas lunch table after dessert.
- **A small jigsaw (a few hundred pieces)** for a quiet Boxing Day.
- **A crossword or puzzle book** matched to their level.
- **Seeds for the garden.** Herbs such as basil like warm weather, which makes them a good fit for a summer stocking. Check the packet's sowing guide for your climate zone.
- **A paperback by an author they already love**, or a slim book of poems, essays or short stories.
- **Bath or shower bombs**, if they have a bath.

One item that could only belong to this person turns a stocking from "nice" into "you thought about me". It doesn't need to be expensive.

- **A keyring, bag tag or luggage tag with their initials.** Good for travellers and anyone who's always misplacing keys.
- **A small item tied to their hobby:** guitar picks, fishing lures, golf tees, a pack of good paintbrushes, a set of knitting stitch markers.
- **A handwritten voucher** for something you'll do: a breakfast, a car wash, a morning of gardening help. These cost nothing and often get mentioned for years.
- **A photo print in a small frame**, from a moment this year.
- **A tin or jar with their name**, filled with their favourite lollies.

If you're ordering anything personalised, check the maker's turnaround and posting dates now. Late November is when small makers' Christmas order books start filling up.

## What to skip

A few categories tend to disappoint adults:

- **Novelty gadgets with a single joke.** Funny for one minute on Christmas morning, then clutter.
- **Generic "pamper sets"** with unfamiliar scents. Scented products are personal; if you don't know what they like, choose unscented or skip it.
- **Anything heat-sensitive that's been sitting in a hot car.** Candles, chocolate and some cosmetics can soften or melt.
- **Multiples of the same small thing** just to fill the space. Tissue paper does that job better.

## Filling the stocking itself

- **Big or heavy items go in first,** at the toe. Small treasures go on top so they're found first.
- **Wrap only the special items.** Unwrapping fifteen tiny parcels is fun for kids but can feel like a chore for adults. Wrap two or three and leave the rest loose.
- **Add a piece of fruit.** An orange in the toe is an old tradition in many families, and a summer stone fruit or a bunch of cherries works just as well in Australia (pop them in at the last minute).
- **Keep a list.** If several people are filling stockings, a shared list in a notes app stops three people buying the same lip balm.

## If you're doing stockings for a whole household

Agreeing on a loose budget per stocking keeps things fair, especially when adults fill each other's. Some families draw names, Kris Kringle-style, so each adult fills just one stocking and can put more thought into it. If that's your plan, our [Kris Kringle ideas](/posts/kris-kringle-gift-ideas-under-20-australia/) suit stockings too.

Above all, pick fewer, better things. Nobody has ever complained that their stocking had *only* five things they loved in it.
